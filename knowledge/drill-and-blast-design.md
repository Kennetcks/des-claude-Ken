# Production drill and blast design (reference values)

Rules of thumb for longhole rises, slots and ring design from the D&B guidelines in the engineer's library: Site G (a contractor), Site H, Site B, Site I, Site J, Site K (a site), Site F and the AMPS D&B training course. Use them to check or draft a ring design. Site values differ, so say which source a number comes from, and the engineer's own rules and the open design always win. Stope shape limits are in [mine-design-standards.md](mine-design-standards.md) and [stope-design.md](stope-design.md).

## Before designing

- No D&B design until the stope note / level extraction plan is approved. Use the latest survey as-builts, flat-backs and benches, CMS of adjacent stopes (overbreak, underbreak) and the void register.
- Know the explosive before you set burden and spacing (ANFO = 100 % bulk energy reference; blow-loaded ANFO ≈ 110 %).
- Allow for deviation: 1° over 30 m moves the toe ≈ 0.5 m. A T45 speed rod with a guide tube is within ≈ 0.5 m at the toe (Site B). Keep hole lengths inside the rig's accurate range.
- Rig envelope: allow 0.8 m left and 1.1 m right of the drill steel near walls (Site B). One rig per panel, named on the plan, never changed. Collar holes into sidewalls between perpendicular and 40° (Site F).
- Never design drilling into previously fired ground without the UGM's consent and a JHA (Site B).

Standard Site B rise patterns: 8-reamer conventional, 6-reamer narrow vein (use the 8-reamer above 10 m), and a vent rise. Site F CO slots: 140 mm eccentric dice-5 (box 1.8 x 1.8 m LHW, centre hole 2 m), 102 mm grid 2.0 x 2.0 m, 89 mm grid 1.6 x 1.6 m; minimum charging collars 3.0 m (140) / 2.0 m (102, 89).

Rise length limits (AMPS): level to level ≈ 30 m, combined uphole / downhole 45–60 m, blind 15–25 m. Rises with top and bottom access can go in 5–10 m lifts: take any structure fully inside one lift, keep a 2:1 cap, stem downholes, then CMS, prep and dip before the next lift.

Burn / rise squares around reamers (Dyno Nobel, used in AMPS): equivalent void φ = d√n for n reamers of diameter d; first square burden a = 1.5 φ, each next square B = W of the previous, W = 1.5 W√2. With 102 mm holes: W₁ ≈ 210 mm, W₂ ≈ 440, W₃ ≈ 930, W₄ ≈ 1,980 mm.

More from the AMPS course:

- Subdrill ≈ 0.5 x burden in competent rock. Standoff 0.25–0.5 x burden from stope walls, 0.5–0.75 x burden from paste, ≥ 1.0 m where toes approach charged or uncharged rings; hanging wall offset ≈ 0.5 x burden, more near structures (or drill through them).
- Ideal break-out angle > 120° for production holes; holes stabbing the hanging wall break at ≈ 90°, so draw an offset line and let the 90° break-out lines meet it halfway between holes.
- Narrow vein collar width (throat) > 1.2 x burden to avoid bridges; collars stabbing the wall ≥ 2 hole diameters apart edge to edge (20–30 cm). A ring burden > 70 % of the blasted collar width can bridge.
- Overlapping toes from upper and lower drives: offset rings by half a burden and overlap 0.5–1.0 m.
- Footwall holes ≥ 1 m long, toes ≥ 0.7–1.0 m apart, 20 cm clearance around the boom. Footwalls flatter than 45° hold ore: strip waste to make 45°.
- Ring dump typically 10–20°, never more than 30° from normal to the face (the bit skips). Stand-up rings change from dumped to vertical at the back of the stope; keep collar separation.
- Stable hanging wall shapes: straight, or concave with paste over it; convex and unsupported concave shapes fail. Undercut profiles should be arched / stepped.
- Reference centreline for ring slicing sits at the rig's dump point height; it sets the section bearing and the distance to pivots / collars. Mark-up: pivot points for horseshoe rigs, collar points for boom rigs, centreline + lasers for narrow vein. Setup distance for a dumped ring: SD = TB − (SH / tan θ₁ − SH / tan θ₂).
- Decking (inert stemming between charges) can replace a standoff, cut charge per delay or fire a multi-lift rise in one blast.

Charging: primer 2 m from the toe, ≤ 10 m apart and staggered ≥ 1 m (primers ≥ 2 m from each other, Site J). Uncharged collars 12–20 x hole diameter and ≥ 1 m; the last ring of a firing keeps ≥ 2.5 m uncharged collars in the backs. Influence radius for 89 mm holes: 1.1 / 1.3 / 1.5 m at 0.8 / 1.0 / 1.2 g/cc.

## Timing

- Rises: start in the centre, fire outwards and speed up towards the outer box. Rule of thumb 20–50 ms per metre of rise for the first delays (Site F diamond 40–50 ms/m), outer box about a third of that.
- Site F LHW delays by length: < 10 m inner box and diamond 400–500 ms, outer box and box cut 100–200 ms; 10–15 m 500–600 / 200–300; > 15 m 600–800 / 300–400. Never below 400 ms (rock can't move) or above 800 ms.
- Site G Staggered H: 200 / 300 / 400 / 500 ms between shot holes for 0–10 / 10–15 / 15–20 / 20–25 m.
- CO slot: 50 ms between holes (100 ms if vibration matters), box cut 100–400 ms, change of direction 200–500 ms.
- Rings: lead hole is the longest charged hole (start downholes first in a 360° ring); 15–25 ms between holes, 20 ms typical; inter-ring 20–50 ms per metre of burden (≥ 100 ms between rings with NONEL, skip 3 numbers).
- Confined firings (void below) go bottom-up and slow; unconfined (void beside) fire faster and throw.
- Keep charge per delay < 1,000 kg (500 kg where vibration is a risk) when doubling up.

## Naming and plans

- Site K rings: R1 is the first ring of the rise, numbered sequentially back through rise, slot and production; inserted rings take a letter suffix (R6A); articulated rings get L / R. Holes H1… left to right; uphole drives start bottom left clockwise, downhole drives top left anticlockwise. Prefix by hole type (E = electrical / comms).
- Site B electronic folders: OREBODY \ LEVEL (the bogging level, not the drilling level) \ PANEL; ring names like `T4_655Nth_PanelA_3`.
- Site F: main rings MR01…, extension rings ER01… numbered outwards from the CO. Pole holes 5 m from the stope edge or brow, three holes 1 m apart and 2 m deep, plus a backup set 5 m behind, unless the collar is > 15 m.
- Plans need peer review and sign-off before issue; never re-issue an unsigned reprint.

## Rhino / boxhole rigs

- Rhino 100: conventional raisebore 1,060 mm (279 mm pilot, ≤ 100 m); uphole boxhole 750 mm single pass ≤ 60 m; downhole 668 mm. Reamer 0.75 m with a 1.3–1.4 m pilot ahead of it, so the pilot reaches ≈ 1.5 m past the reamed length.
- Rig: drill module 6.65 m rigid (laser lines to collar 6.65 m), width 3.1 m, dump ±15°, rotation ±30° (head sticks out 1.04 m uphole / 1.3 m downhole), max gradient 1:8, 70 t thrust needs a solid floor. Cuttings chute reaches 3.1–3.67 m, so collar ≥ 5 m from the drive edge. Cuttings ≈ 0.5 m³ per metre (750 mm). Holes > 25 m need extra rod racks beside the rig.
- Design angles from the rig's right side: 90° vertical, limits 60° and 120°. In a 4-way, keep it near vertical. Drill the boxhole before slot development around it. Deswik has a "Rhino" rig template; survey needs laser lines, a vertical alignment line (offset with the pivot) and the collar point; mark up in a different paint colour.

## Development faces (AMPS)

- Size drives for their purpose and the equipment (jumbo rail and bolt length, longhole carousel and boom, loader, truck turning); 1:50 for drainage. Gradient changes take a couple of rounds.
- Burn: more / larger reamers give more void and a better pull but cost drilling time; over-drill reamers past the shot hole; place the burn in the rock that blasts best and fire across structure, not along it. Holes drilled slightly up so water and cuttings drain; tighter, downward lifters on the bottom two rows.
- Perimeter holes: lower-energy or decoupled charges, tighter spacing, offset from the final profile; increase the offset near structures.
- Overbreak fixes: weaker or half-loaded perimeter holes, decoupling, fire perimeter rows separately or as pre / post split, larger perimeter offset or second-last-row distance, uncharged perimeter holes.
- Short pulls: reamers not full depth, poor burn, wrong timing, wet ANFO, holes not blown clean. Non-square faces: square the face, adjust hole lengths on corners, adjust pattern to the rock.
- Turnouts: mine 1–2 cuts past the turnout before taking it (look at the ground, install cables first, square the wall; in declines the extra cuts make a sump or stockpile). Avoid 4-ways; cable intersections progressively.
- Match cut length to the cycle (≈ 85–90 % of the blasting cycle) and to the mesh / bolt pattern; bolts within 10° of perpendicular.

## QAQC and reconciliation

- Drilling QAQC: daily inspections, rig angle checks, collar (and toe) pickups, in-hole surveys, breakthrough checks, drill returns (depths, undrilled, redrills, moved collars, broken ground, water).
- Blasting QAQC: by law in WA a hole must be blown clean before charging; hole prep plans with the drill returns; charge plan returns, planned vs actual explosives, walk the shot.
- Stope reconciliation: planned tonnes and grade vs CMS, tonnes bogged, sampled grade and plant; report underbreak and overbreak by wall.
- Hazards to design around: breakthroughs (water, paste, services, people), drilling into fired ground (WA: never within 15 cm of a butt), un-grouted diamond holes, sulphide dust, open holes and brows. Stope brow starts at the last row of complete ground support; stope bund wall to wall ≥ 1.5 m high, charge bund ≥ 2 m.
